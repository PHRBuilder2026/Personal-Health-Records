Do I Need a PHR?

Your complete health record — in your hands.

Why You Need This

Modern healthcare is fragmented. You see different doctors, take different tests, and pick up prescriptions from different pharmacies — and nobody but you has the complete picture. PHR puts that picture in one place, on your iPad.

Become an active participant in your own care

When you keep your own record, you walk into every appointment knowing what's on file, what changed since last time, and what questions still need answers. You stop being a passive participant.

Use modern AI tools to make sense of your data

Ask AI and Health Check connect your record to Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, or xAI (Grok) — and Ask AI also works with Perplexity in Safari — for explanations and analysis without you having to retype your situation from scratch every time.

Combine records from every provider

Pull data from any patient portal that exports CCDA files (most do). Lab results from one clinic, medications from another, immunizations from a travel doctor — all combined in one record you control.

Instant access in an emergency

You don't have to remember every medication, condition, or recent procedure in a stressful moment. A printed report — or your iPad with PHR open — has it all in front of you. Share a copy with your spouse or a trusted family member and they can speak for you when you can't.

Manage care for your whole family

Up to 10 patients per device. Switch between household members in a tap. One app keeps the kids' immunization records, your aging parents' medications, and your own labs all close at hand.

Share on your terms

Print, AirDrop, email, or hand off as a PDF or Excel file. PHR doesn't sit between you and your data — it just makes the data easier to take with you.

Who This Is For — and Who It Isn't

PHR works hardest for people who:

  • See more than one provider or use more than one health system
  • Travel away from their home health system
  • Manage records for a partner, parent, or child as well as themselves
  • Have ongoing conditions, regular medications, or recurring tests to track over time
  • Want to be active participants in their own healthcare

If you see a single doctor at a single health system, don't travel, only manage your own care, and don't have ongoing health concerns to track, you may not need a personal health record at all. That's a fine answer — your provider's portal probably covers what you need.
