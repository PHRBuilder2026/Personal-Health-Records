Backing Up Your Railroad — and Getting It Back

Backing Up Your Railroad — and Getting It Back

Model Railroad Record Keeper keeps everything on your iPad. Nothing automatically syncs in the background — good for speed and privacy, and it also means the roster you've spent many hours building has exactly one copy. A backup turns "the only copy" into "one of several."

Pick your two places

Under Settings you can point the app at two backup locations: a folder on the iPad, one in iCloud Drive, or a thumb drive that's plugged in. One is enough. Two is better when the second lives somewhere the iPad doesn't — an iPad that goes missing takes any backups stored on it with it.

Making a backup

Backup All Files saves a complete copy — everything the app holds, plus your attached manuals — as a dated folder. You can send it to either location or both at once, and if a location isn't available, the app names the one that missed out instead of quietly reporting success. Two catches: a thumb drive only counts while it's plugged in, and an iCloud backup may still be uploading for a few minutes after the app says it's done.

The app also counts your edits and nudges you when it's been a while. It'll suggest thinning out old backups once they pile up, but it never deletes one for you.

Getting it back

  1. Pick the backup folder you want.
  2. Check the record-count comparison — what's on the iPad now against what's in that backup.
  3. Confirm. Your current data is set aside as a safety copy first.
  4. Fully close the app and reopen it; the restore finishes on the way back up.

A damaged or newer-version backup is refused rather than half-applied, and if a restored copy won't open, your previous data comes back on its own. Undo Last Restore appears afterward — the button you want when you've restored the wrong night's backup.

Moving to a new iPad is the same two steps: full backup on the old one, restore on the new.
