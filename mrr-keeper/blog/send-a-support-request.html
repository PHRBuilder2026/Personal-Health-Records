Send a Support Request — Even When Nothing's Broken

Send a Support Request — Even When Nothing's Broken

There's an envelope icon in the banner of every screen in Model Railroad Record Keeper. Most people assume it's for when something has gone wrong. It is — but it's also the only way I hear from you, and "nothing's broken, here's how it's going" is worth just as much to me as a bug report.

How to send one

Tap the envelope from whatever screen you're on. Which screen you were on rides along with the message, so I know where you were without you having to describe it. Pick an issue type — Bug Report, Feature Request, Question, or Other — type your note, and Send hands it to Mail so it goes out from your own email account. A copy of everything you've sent stays under Settings → Support Tickets.

If a picture would say it faster: copy a screenshot, then tap Paste Image. Up to three. When Mail asks about image size, choose Actual Size — the smaller options blur the text.

What's worth telling me

Anything, honestly. A few things help more than others:

  • What you're using the app for — inventory, operating sessions, or both.
  • Something that took longer than it should have, or that you went looking for and didn't find.
  • A place where the app made you do it its way instead of yours.
  • What you'd change first if it were your app.

Bugs too, of course. If something went wrong, what you were doing right before it happened is the single most useful sentence you can write.

Two things I'm weighing

I built this around my own operating experience over the last several years, which is a sample size of one. Two ideas are sitting on my list and I'd rather hear from you than guess.

Car cards — printed cards and waybills for a session, generated from the roster you've already entered instead of filled in by hand.

Your own colors — picking the colors used for your railroads instead of taking the ones the app hands out.

If either of those would change how you use the app, tell me. And if there's something you'd rather have before either one, that's better still — the list gets built from what people ask for.
