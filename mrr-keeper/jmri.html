How Record Keeper works alongside JMRI.

On the App Store

Two apps, two different jobs

If you're already running JMRI, keep running it — Model Railroad Record Keeper isn't a replacement, and it doesn't try to be. JMRI controls your railroad. Record Keeper documents it. Most JMRI layouts we've seen also have a three-ring binder, a shoebox of receipts, and four spreadsheets that haven't matched each other since 2019. That's the job Record Keeper takes over.

JMRI manages the trains that run. Record Keeper documents everything you own to make them run.

What JMRI does (and does well)

JMRI is free, open-source software that's been the hobby's standard for computer control for over two decades. DecoderPro programs your DCC decoders and manages a roster. PanelPro builds control panels that throw real turnouts. OperationsPro builds trains and prints manifests and switch lists. Its WiThrottle server lets phone throttle apps run your trains. It talks to more than twenty DCC systems, and it costs nothing.

The trade-off is what JMRI asks of you: a computer connected to your layout, a Java installation, and a real learning curve. It's control software — it's deepest where the wires are.

What Record Keeper does

Record Keeper is a $9.99 iPad and Mac app with no Java, no connection to the layout, and nothing to configure. Use it in the recliner, at the workbench, or at a train show — no laptop parked beside the layout. Everything lives on your own device — no cloud, no account, no data collected.

Your whole collection, not just the roster

JMRI keeps a roster of what runs. It has no home for everything else you've bought for the hobby — structures and kits, track and turnouts, decoders and wire, tools, scenery and detail parts — or what you paid, what it's worth, what's running low, and what's for sale.

  • Hobby inventory with quantities, prices, values, storage spots and a low-stock warning.
  • Your eBay purchases, brought in — download your history from eBay and it becomes inventory, sorted by your own rules.
  • A reference library — manuals as PDFs, saved links and notes, all offline.

Locomotives and rolling stock, with the paperwork

Every engine and car with its photo, marks, specs, couplers, what you paid and what it's worth — plus the DCC side you set up in DecoderPro, written down where you can find it at the workbench.

  • Decoder, address, sound card and stay-alive, and the CVs you changed — ready for the next reset.
  • F1–F32 labels that fill in from the sound card, and a Function Key Matrix that prints every engine's keys on one sheet.
  • Custom column views and a pivot table — count or total your fleet by type, road or year bought, and print exactly what's on screen.
  • Car cards you design, with a fold-up waybill pocket, and fold-over waybills.
  • Bring your JMRI roster along — locomotives and cars import from JMRI CSV files.

Yard Office — switch lists without a computer at the layout

Run real operations on paper or on the iPad. Your industries ask for cars, you build the train, Record Keeper prints the switch list and keeps track of where every car sits afterward.

  • Car Location — every car at every spot, loaded or empty, and Where's That Car? to find one in seconds.
  • Routes and industry orders — each industry asks for what it needs set out and picked up.
  • Build Train and Dispatch — pick a route, the orders come in, the cars are chosen, the switch list prints. Key in what the crew did and the car locations update.
  • Quick Switch List for a short job, and a live operating board for the session — who's ready, who's running, which tracks are locked.

Where they overlap — honestly

OperationsPro and Record Keeper both build trains and print switch lists, in different ways. OperationsPro simulates car forwarding: you set up schedules and loads, and the computer decides which cars move and where. Record Keeper keeps the decisions with you — your industries' orders, your routes, your call. If you love OperationsPro's automatic car routing, keep it. If you'd rather decide the moves yourself and keep the paperwork simple, Record Keeper may fit better.

DecoderPro also keeps function-key labels. Record Keeper keeps them too, next to the purchase record, the photo and the CVs — on the iPad in your hand rather than the computer by the layout.

Use both. JMRI at the command station; Record Keeper everywhere else.
