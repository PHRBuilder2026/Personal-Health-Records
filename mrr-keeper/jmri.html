JMRI runs your railroad. Record Keeper remembers it.

Two apps, two different jobs

If you're already running JMRI, keep running it — Model Railroad Record Keeper isn't a replacement, and it doesn't try to be. JMRI controls your railroad. Record Keeper documents it. Most JMRI layouts we've seen also have a three-ring binder, a shoebox of receipts, and four spreadsheets that haven't matched each other since 2019. That's the job Record Keeper takes over.

JMRI manages the trains that run. Record Keeper documents everything you own to make them run.

What JMRI does (and does well)

JMRI is free, open-source software that's been the hobby's standard for computer control for over two decades. DecoderPro programs your DCC decoders and manages a roster. PanelPro builds control panels that throw real turnouts. OperationsPro builds trains and prints manifests and switch lists. Its WiThrottle server lets phone throttle apps run your trains. It talks to more than twenty DCC systems, and it costs nothing.

The trade-off is what JMRI asks of you: a computer connected to your layout, a Java installation, and a real learning curve. It's control software — it's deepest where the wires are.

What Record Keeper does

Record Keeper is a $9.99 iPad and Mac app with no Java, no connection to the layout, and nothing to configure. Use it in the recliner, at the workbench, or at a train show — no laptop parked beside the layout.

Your whole collection, not just the roster

JMRI keeps a roster of the locomotives and cars that run. It has no home for everything else you've bought for the hobby — the structures and kits, the track and turnouts, the decoders and wire, the tools, the scenery and detail parts — or for what you paid, what it's worth, what's running low, and what's for sale. That's Record Keeper's Inventory, and it's the part nothing else in the hobby does.

It also keeps the rest of the records JMRI doesn't:

  • Hobby inventory beyond the rails — track, electronics, structures, kits, tools, scenery — with quantities, what you paid, what it's worth, and a warning when you're running low.
  • Your eBay purchases, brought in — download your purchase history from eBay and Record Keeper turns it into inventory items, sorted by your own rules, so years of buying don't have to be typed in.
  • A full equipment roster with the paperwork side — what you paid, what it's worth now, condition, where it's stored, when it was last serviced, and a photo. Locomotives carry their DCC details too: address, decoder, function keys, and the CVs you've changed — so what you programmed in DecoderPro is written down somewhere you can find at the workbench.
  • Your JMRI roster comes with you — Record Keeper imports locomotives and cars from JMRI CSV files, so an established roster moves over without retyping.
  • A reference library — manuals as attached PDFs, saved links, and notes, all offline on your iPad or Mac.
  • A project to-do list built for layout work, with priorities, categories, and saved views.
  • Operating session paperwork — jobs with steps and dependencies, a dispatch board for running a session with real people, engineer assignments, and printed job cards and schedules. Dozens of reports print from the iPad or Mac, or export as PDFs.
  • Car cards and waybills — design your own car cards, print them with a fold-up waybill pocket, and print fold-over waybills sized to fit. Give each industry what it ships and receives, and Record Keeper drafts the waybills for you.
  • Switch lists and car locations — record where every car sits, build a train from a route and your industries’ orders (or a quick list of cars), print the switch list, and key in what the crew did. Record Keeper updates where every car sits.

Everything lives on your own iPad or Mac — no cloud, no account, no data collected.

Where they overlap — honestly

OperationsPro and Record Keeper both handle operating sessions, in different ways. OperationsPro simulates car forwarding: you set up schedules and loads, and the computer decides which cars move and where. Record Keeper keeps the decisions with you. Your industries ask for cars through orders, you build each train from a route, and Record Keeper prints the switch list and keeps track of where every car sits afterward. It also prints car cards and waybills if your crew prefers cards in a pocket, and it’s the dispatcher’s clipboard during the session — who’s ready, who’s running, which tracks are locked. If you love OperationsPro’s automatic car routing, keep it. If you’d rather decide the moves yourself and keep the paperwork simple, Record Keeper may fit better.
