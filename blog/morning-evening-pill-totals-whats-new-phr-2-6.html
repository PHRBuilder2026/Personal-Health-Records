Morning and Evening Pill Totals: What's New in PHR 2.6

A few small things you asked for

PHR 2.6 is a small release built around a few requests. Your medication list can now add up the pills you take in the morning and in the evening. Sleep nights can carry your own notes. And a couple of screens got easier to read.

Morning and evening totals

PHR 2.5 added optional AM and PM columns to the Medications list, showing how many pills each medication needs in the morning and in the evening. Now, whenever your view includes either column, a Total row sits at the top of the list and adds them up.

  • The total counts only what's showing, so your Status and Type filters apply. Set Status to Active and you'll see what you take today.
  • As Needed medications don't add to the count.
  • The same Total row prints at the top of the Custom View report, which makes a handy sheet for filling a pill organizer.

To turn the columns on, open Views on the Medications list, choose Customize, and switch on AM and PM.

What those little icons mean

The Medications list shows small icons after a medication's name: one for discontinued, one for a bad reaction, one for refills left, and a few more. The Help for that screen now opens with a key that shows each icon in its own colour, with a few words on what it means. Tap the ? in the upper-right of the Medications list to see it.

A note for any night

Sleep Study Daily now lets you add your own note to a night. Look for + Note on the Sleep Stages line. Write down anything that might explain the night: a late dinner, a new medication, a long walk. The start of the note shows on the line, and the full text is a tap away. To remove a note, clear the text and save.

Notes are kept separately from your imported sleep data, so bringing in new data from Apple Health never touches them.

Notes on the BP History Report

On the Blood Pressure History Report, a reading's note now prints on its own line under the reading instead of squeezing into a narrow column. Longer notes are finally easy to read on paper.
