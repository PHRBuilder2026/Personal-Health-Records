A Summer of Updates: Printing, Ask AI, and Safer Imports

What's Arrived Since July

It's been a while since our last post, and PHR has kept moving. Here's a roundup of what's arrived since July — printouts for your doctor, Ask AI that reads your own files, and imports that leave your edits alone.

Print it for your next appointment

The Blood Pressure Tracker and Glucose Tracker each have a Print button. What prints is exactly what's on screen — the same dates, the same sort — with a summary at the top: the count, your average, and how many readings fell outside range. Out-of-range readings are flagged with arrows that still read clearly in black and white.

The graphs print too, whichever view you're on: the trend chart, the day-by-day logbook, or the in-range breakdown. A few more:

  • Sleep Study — the Daily Trend chart prints with its summary of nights, average, lowest, and highest.
  • Lab results — a new Not OK filter in Print Selections narrows a print to just the results outside their normal range.

Everything prints as a PDF, so you can put it on paper, email it, or save it to Files.

Ask AI about your ECG — and other files

Open an Apple Watch ECG recording, tap Question, and send it to Ask AI. The recording's data goes along automatically, so the answer is about your trace, not ECGs in general.

On any question, the paperclip next to Ask AI lets you attach a PDF, an image, or a text file up to 5 MB — a lab report, a discharge summary, a photo of a printed result. This works with the in-app AI services: Claude, OpenAI, Google Gemini, and xAI. Answers can also run longer now, and PHR tells you if one was cut short.

AI answers are background reading, not medical advice. The best use of one is a sharper question for your doctor.

Fix an import once, and it stays fixed

When you edit an imported record by hand, PHR now remembers it. Re-importing won't overwrite your version, merges keep dates you typed, and bulk deletes can skip the records you've edited.

Smaller things worth knowing

  • Blood pressure entry remembers how you last took the reading, and the Now button sets both the date and the time.
  • Renaming a condition, facility, or provider updates every record that pointed to the old name.
  • You can read a condition's note without opening the condition.
  • The Help Index follows the same order as the sidebar, with groups you can collapse.
  • Restoring a backup no longer resets account settings it didn't include.
